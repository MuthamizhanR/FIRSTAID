## Acute cystitis


[[Inflammation|Inflammation]] of urinary bladder. Presents as suprapubic pain, dysuria, urinary frequency, urgency. Systemic signs (eg, high fever, chills) are usually absent.

Risk factors include female sex (short urethra), sexual intercourse, indwelling catheter, diabetes mellitus, impaired bladder emptying.

Causes:

- _E coli_ (most common)

- _Staphylococcus saprophyticus_ —seen in sexually active young women ( _E coli_ is still more common in this group)

- _Klebsiella_

- _Proteus mirabilis_ —urine has ammonia scent

- _Enterococcus_ spp.

Labs:<sup>⊕</sup> leukocyte esterase.<sup>⊕</sup> nitrites (indicates presence of Enterobacteriaceae). Sterile pyuria (pyuria with<sup>⊝</sup> urine cultures) could suggest urethritis by _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_ or _Chlamydia trachomatis_ .

Treatment: antibiotics (eg, TMP-SMX, nitrofurantoin).
